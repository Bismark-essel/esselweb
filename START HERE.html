Your website

Essel De Photographer — how to use it, how to put it online, and what is still waiting for you.

This was built for you at no charge. Everything that could be done without you is done: the website, the editor, the search setup, the security, and a test suite that checks ninety things every time anything changes.

What is left is the part only you can do. Your photographs. Your words. Your Google listing. Nobody can supply those, and a photographer's website with five pictures on it is a website that will not win you work. Read this once, all the way through, before you touch anything. It will take about twenty minutes and it will save you a week.

1Look at it first

Find the folder you were sent. Inside it is a file called index.html. Double-click it. Your website opens in your browser.

Nothing is installed, nothing is running, nothing is online. You are looking at files on your own computer. You can do this on any machine, for ever, even with no internet.

Click around. The address bar will say something starting with file:/// — that is normal and it means the website is on your computer and nowhere else yet.

The one thing to understand about this website

It is just files. There is no database, no subscription, no account that can expire, no monthly bill. If every company involved disappeared tomorrow, you would still have this folder and it would still work.

That is deliberate. Keep a copy of this folder on a hard drive or a pen drive. If you ever lose everything else, that copy is your website.

2The editor

You change the website through a file called admin.html. It is in the same folder. Double-click it.

Use Chrome or Edge. Not Safari, not Firefox.

The editor saves straight into your folder, and only Chrome and Edge let a page do that. In Safari it will open but it cannot save, which is worse than not opening at all.

If Chrome is not your normal browser: right-click admin.html → Open With → Google Chrome.

The first thing it shows is a button that says Open the website folder. Press it, and choose the folder that contains index.html — the same folder the editor itself is sitting in. Your browser will ask if you are sure you want to let the page edit files. Say yes. That permission is what makes the whole thing work.

Across the top are five tabs:

TabWhat it is for
PhotographsAdd pictures, write what is in them, set which part stays in frame, choose which appear on the home page
CollectionsThe four groups your work is sorted into — Portrait, Events, Corporate, Editorial. Rename them, describe them, add a new one

Do this now, before anything else

Open the editor, change one word in the About text, press Save, refresh the website and see it change. Then change it back.

Once you have done that once, you own this website. Until you have done it once, you do not.

3How to prepare photographs

This is the part people get wrong, and getting it wrong is the difference between a site that looks expensive and one that looks cheap.

Export at 2000 pixels on the long edge

Not 6000. The website builds four sizes from what you give it and throws the rest away, so a huge file is a long wait for nothing. Not 800 either — that is already too small for a laptop screen and it will look soft.

Kind of workShapeExport at
Portrait4:52000 × 2500
Events, Corporate, Editorial3:22000 × 1333
Film stills16:92000 × 1125
Hero, for computerswide2400 × 1350
Hero, for phonestall1200 × 2133

Four rules

  1. High-quality JPEG, sRGB, and do not compress it first. The editor makes the web version itself. Compressing a picture twice makes skin look like plastic. You are a photographer — that is an insult to your own work.
  2. Crop to the shape before you export. Every picture keeps its own shape, so one 3:2 frame in a row of 4:5 frames leaves a step in the grid. 4:5 and 3:4 look almost the same and are not — crop exactly.
  3. Set the focal point on every portrait. In the editor, click the face. Without it the website keeps the middle of the picture, and faces are usually in the upper third — so on a narrow screen you get a beautifully framed chest.
  4. Write what is in the picture. One short line. Google reads it, and so does anyone using a screen reader. "Bride laughing outside the church, Accra" is right. "IMG_4471" is not.

Never send your masters to the website

Your full-resolution files are what you sell. Keep them on your own drive. If you ever put them in a folder called originals inside the website folder, the setup already blocks that folder from being published — but the real protection is simply not putting them there.

4What is still empty

Checked on the day this was written. Everything here is something only you can fill in.

You have five photographs on a photography website

One Portrait, one Events, one Corporate, two Editorial. That is the single biggest thing standing between this site and work coming in. A visitor who sees four pictures assumes you have taken four pictures.

Aim for eight to twelve in each collection. Not your whole archive — your best twelve. A portfolio is judged by its weakest picture, not its strongest, so anything you are unsure about should be left out.

No collection has a cover picture

The Collections page shows a card for each of the four. Each card wants one photograph that represents the whole collection. Set it in the editor, on the Collections tab. Until then the cards show a labelled empty box — honest, but it does not sell anything.

Only one hero picture has a phone version

There are five big pictures at the top of the home page. Each one wants two files: a wide one for computers and a tall one for phones. Only one of the five has its tall version. Most of the people who look at your work are on a phone, so this is more important than it sounds. See the sizes in section 3.

No showcase picture is chosen

On each collection page, one photograph can open out to full width as somebody scrolls. It is the nicest thing on the site and it is switched off, because it has to be your choice which picture deserves it. One per collection, set in the editor.

No films

The Film section exists and is empty. Each film is one still picture plus a link — there is no video player on the site, deliberately, because an embedded player loads half a megabyte of somebody else's code and tracks every visitor before anyone presses play. Add a still and a link and it works.

The About text is ours, not yours

It was written from what we knew about you, to have something real on the page instead of a grey box. Read it and change it into your own words. It is the second-most-read page on any photographer's site and it is the one that sounds like a person or does not.

Two things to check with your own eyes

The logo lettering. The logo was traced from a file that had been named "Yossel". The name inside the file has been corrected, but nobody has confirmed that the drawing itself spells Essel De Photographer correctly. Open images/logo.svg, look at it, and tell Patrick if a letter is wrong. This is your name — check it.

The domain. The website is built for esseldephotographer.com. If you have not actually bought that address, section 6 is where you do it — and if you buy a different one, tell Patrick, because it is written in one place and has to match.

5Putting it on the internet: GitHub

Two free services, and you need both. Here is what each one is for, in one line each:

  • GitHub keeps your files and remembers every version of them. It is the filing cabinet.
  • Netlify takes those files and serves them to the world at a real web address. It is the shopfront.

They connect once, and after that everything is automatic: you put new files in the filing cabinet and the shopfront updates itself within a minute.

Step by step

  1. Go to github.com and make a free account. Use your working email.
  2. Press the + at the top right → New repository. A repository is just a folder that remembers its own history.
  3. Name it essel-de-photographer. Choose Private. Do not tick "Add a README". Press Create repository.
  4. On the next page, find the link that says uploading an existing file. Click it.
  5. Open your website folder on your computer. Select everything inside it — not the folder itself, the things inside it — and drag them onto the GitHub page.
  6. Wait for the upload to finish. It will take a few minutes because the photographs are the big part.
  7. At the bottom, press Commit changes.

Do not upload these three

START HERE.html — this file. It is for you, not the public.

The originals folder, if you ever make one.

node_modules, if it ever appears. It is a folder of machinery, not of website.

Everything else goes up, including all the photographs.

There are faster ways to do this with typed commands. You do not need them, and neither does Patrick — dragging files in the browser is the whole method here, on purpose, because it works on any computer without installing anything.

6Netlify, and your domain

  1. Go to netlify.com. Press Sign up and choose Sign up with GitHub — that way the two already know each other.
  2. Press Add new site → Import an existing project → GitHub.
  3. It will ask permission to see your repositories. Allow it, and pick essel-de-photographer.
  4. It will ask for a build command and a publish directory. Leave the build command empty and set the publish directory to a single dot: .
    There is already a file called netlify.toml in your folder that says exactly this, so it should fill itself in. If it does, leave it alone.
  5. Press Deploy. Wait about a minute.

You now have a live website at an address like random-words-12345.netlify.app. Open it on your phone. That is your site, on the internet, for free.

Your own address

  1. Buy the domain. Namecheap, Porkbun and Cloudflare all sell them; expect to pay for a year at a time. Buy it in your own name, with your own email and your own card. A domain somebody else owns is a business somebody else can switch off.
  2. In Netlify: Domain management → Add a domain → type it in.
  3. Netlify will show you either two nameservers or a couple of records to copy. Go to wherever you bought the domain and paste them in.
  4. Wait. It can take minutes or it can take most of a day. This is normal and there is nothing to fix while you wait.
  5. When it is live, check the address bar says https and shows a padlock. Netlify sets that up by itself and it is free.

Write these down somewhere safe

The email and password for GitHub, for Netlify, for the domain seller, and for the Google account you use in the next two sections. Four logins. Losing the domain login is the only one that is genuinely painful to recover.

From now on, changing the website is three steps

  1. Edit in admin.html and press Save.
  2. Drag the changed files into GitHub, the same way as before. It is usually data/content.js and whatever new files appeared in photos.
  3. Netlify notices and updates the live site by itself, in about a minute.

7Google Business Profile

This is free, it takes half an hour, and for a photographer in Accra it will probably bring you more work than the website does on its own. It is what puts you in the map when somebody searches photographer near me.

  1. Go to google.com/business and sign in with a Google account you will keep.
  2. Enter your business name exactly as you want it read: Essel De Photographer. Spell it the same everywhere, for ever — Google matches businesses partly on the name being identical across the internet.
  3. Choose the category Photographer. Add Portrait studio, Wedding photographer, Event photographer as extra categories if they are true.
  4. If you go to clients rather than having a studio they visit, say so, and set your service area — Accra and wherever else you actually work.
  5. Put in your phone number and your website address. Use the same phone number that is on the website.
  6. Google will verify you, usually by video or by post. Do it properly; an unverified profile does not show.

Then use it, which is the part people skip

  • Put photographs on it. Profiles with pictures get far more attention than profiles without. Post a few every month. Google treats a profile that is being updated as a business that is open.
  • Ask every happy client for a review. Not a vague ask — send them the direct review link the moment you deliver their photographs, while they are pleased. This is the single biggest thing you control. Reviews are the main reason one photographer appears above another.
  • Reply to every review, good and bad. A calm reply to a complaint reads better to the next customer than no complaint at all.
  • Never buy reviews. Google is good at spotting them and the punishment is your profile disappearing.

Why the website has no map on it. An embedded Google map drops tracking cookies on your visitors before they have agreed to anything, which matters under Ghana's Data Protection Act, and it is one more thing that can break. The contact page prints your address as text and links out to the map instead — which opens the map app the visitor already has, with their own location in it, in one tap. It is better for them and safer for you.

8Google Search Console

Search Console is Google's own report on your website: what people searched for to find you, which pages they landed on, and anything broken. Free, and it is the only honest source for that information.

  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console and sign in with the same Google account.
  2. Press Add property and choose Domain. Type your domain with no https:// and no www — just esseldephotographer.com.
  3. It will give you one line of text called a TXT record. Copy it, go to wherever you bought your domain, find DNS settings, and add it as a new TXT record.
  4. Go back and press Verify. If it fails, wait an hour and press it again — DNS changes are slow, and that is the usual reason.

Submit your sitemap

A sitemap is a list of every page on your site, written for search engines so they do not have to guess. Yours already exists — it is the file sitemap.xml in your folder, and it was written for you. You just have to tell Google where it is.

  1. In Search Console, click Sitemaps in the left menu.
  2. In the box, type sitemap.xml and press Submit.
  3. It will say "Success" or "Couldn't fetch". If it says couldn't fetch, wait a day and look again — it very often sorts itself out.

Then press Request indexing on your home page: in the bar at the top, paste your home page address, wait for it to check, and press Request indexing. That asks Google to come and look now rather than whenever it gets round to it.

Your site is already set up to be found

Indexing is on. The file that tells search engines what they may look at (robots.txt) says yes to everything except the editor. The sitemap is written. Every page carries a proper title and description. None of that needs doing again.

What it cannot do is invent content. Google ranks pages with things on them. Go back to section 4.

Expect nothing for a while

A new website with a new domain is not going to appear on the first page for anything competitive for months. This is normal, it is not broken, and nobody who promises otherwise is telling you the truth. What moves it: photographs, your Google Business Profile, real reviews, and time.

9What to do every month

  • Add your best few photographs from the month's work. Ten minutes in the editor.
  • Post two or three pictures to your Google Business Profile.
  • Ask every client you delivered to for a review, with the direct link.
  • Open Search Console and look at what people actually searched for to reach you. It is usually not what you expect, and it tells you what to photograph and what to write next.
  • Check the site on your own phone. You will spot things there you never see on a laptop.

10When something looks wrong

What you seeWhat it usually is
The editor opens but will not save You are in Safari or Firefox. Open it in Chrome or Edge.
You saved, but the website looks the same Refresh the page. If it is still the same, hold Shift and refresh — your browser is showing you a remembered copy.
You changed it, but the live site is old You saved on your computer but did not drag the changed files into GitHub. Saving and publishing are two different things.
A picture looks soft or fuzzy It was exported too small, or compressed before it was uploaded. Export again at 2000px from the original.

An empty labelled box where a picture should be That is the site being honest — nothing has been put there yet. It is a to-do, not a fault.
Google still cannot find you Almost always time. Check in Search Console that the sitemap says Success, then wait.

Before you ask for help

Say what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead — and send a screenshot. Those three things turn an hour of guessing into five minutes.

One last thing

This site is deliberately built to depend on nothing. No plugins to update, no subscription to forget, no theme that breaks, no database to go down. That is why it will still work in five years, and it is also why nobody can hold it hostage — the whole thing is in a folder you own.

The other side of that bargain is that it will only ever be as good as what you put into it. There is no template to hide behind and no stock photograph anywhere on it. Every picture on that site will be one you took.

Start with section 4. Twelve photographs per collection. Everything else follows from that.
